01The honest problem

The layers are measured. The body underneath is not.

August was spent making the anatomy sit correctly on the body instead of being placed by eye. Every organ now lines up with the vertebra it belongs to, and an internal audit went from six of eight organs out of position to zero of eight. That work is real, and it is the reason the treatment points land where they land.

But it does not fix the foundation. The body itself is a generated mesh, not one derived from scan data. The skeleton, organs and muscles sitting inside it were built to fit that mesh. Measuring carefully against a cartoon still leaves you a cartoon, and no amount of further layering changes that.

Open the working viewer

Judge it yourself before reading the rest. Turn it, switch to the female body, toggle x-ray. Everything I am about to say about the foundation will be easier to see with it open.

02See one for yourself

A real model, in your browser, right now

Rather than describe the difference, here is one you can turn and take apart yourself. This is BodyParts3D, built by the Database Center for Life Science in Japan from real anatomical data. It is the dataset the open anatomy world is built on. No account, no install.

Open the live model

It is a research tool, so the interface is plain. Look past that at the geometry. Every structure has a real shape and a real relationship to the ones around it, because it came from a body rather than from a sculptor.

03Three ways to get a real base

What the options actually are

Option A · the commercial standard

Zygote

Their collection is built outward from a full skeleton derived from CT scans, with every other system fitted to it. Roughly 9.9 million polygons, delivered in the formats we already work in, with male and female both complete.

They license for embedding in distributed software on a royalty or subscription basis, which is the arrangement we would need to ship this inside an (se) app. Pricing is quoted, not listed.

See the collection →

Option B · the fastest path

BioDigital Human

A finished platform with a viewer API and mobile SDKs. It would stand up quickest, and their anatomy is genuinely excellent.

The tradeoff is ownership. You would be renting their viewer and their platform, and the meridian and treatment point overlays would have to live inside their terms rather than ours. Their explore view also needs a free account before it will open, so it is a sign-up rather than a click.

See the platform →

Option C · free, with a catch

Z-Anatomy

An open atlas built on that same BodyParts3D data, free to download and genuinely good. If cost were the only question this would be the answer.

The licence is the problem. It carries a Creative Commons ShareAlike term, which requires anything built on top of it to be published under the same open licence. For franchise intellectual property that is a trap worth naming now rather than discovering after the work is done. I would rule it out for anything (se) ships, and keep it only for testing.

04The part I do not want to soften

What carries over, and what does not

Swapping the base is not free, and you should know the cost going in rather than after.

Carries overThe meridian and treatment point placement, because it is derived from measured landmarks rather than hardcoded coordinates. Re-probe a new body, and the points resolve against it.
Carries overThe verification suite. Every check that proves a point sits on skin, an organ sits at its vertebra, or the file runs offline.
Carries overThe viewer itself: the layer controls, the x-ray mode, the organ clock, the region cards, and the film pipeline that produced the walkthroughs.
Does notThe organs, skeleton and muscles built this summer. Real geometry replaces them outright. That is a meaningful share of what August paid for, and a swap writes it off.
05Why it matters for (se)

Points that land on real landmarks

Credibility with cliniciansA provider looking at the current model can tell it is illustrative. On a scan-derived skeleton, the treatment points sit on bone landmarks that a practitioner recognises, which is the difference between a teaching tool and a graphic.
The poster becomes possibleThe numbered acupoints are defined proportionally against real bone landmarks. A real skeleton is what makes placing them defensible rather than approximate.
It stops being an experimentRight now every improvement is another layer on the same foundation. Changing the foundation once ends that pattern.
06What I would do

Park it, or price it. Not both.

My honest recommendation is the one in my email: park the anatomy model for September and put the hours into the education and franchise onboarding gaps, which are the more valuable problem and closer to what you brought me in for.

If the model does come back, it should come back as its own decision with a licensing number attached, not as an open line on the retainer. I can get quotes from Zygote and BioDigital in a few days. That turns this from an open-ended spend into a figure you can say yes or no to.

Either answer works

Tell me to get the quotes, or tell me to shelve it and point September at the content gaps. Both are good outcomes. The only bad one is spending another month layering.
