01Where the current site falls short

Three remarkable venues, one website from 2011

  • The design predates the smartphones couples now shop venues on; photos render small and dated on the screens that matter most.
  • Serenity Overlook, your St. Thomas venue, appears nowhere except a contact-form dropdown. Couples dreaming of a Caribbean wedding cannot find it.
  • The details that book weddings, the walnut tree, the rose garden or woodland choice, the mancave, the vintage car, are buried in paragraphs or missing entirely.
  • Inquiries arrive through a generic form with no date-first flow, and couples get no instant reply while they are still excited.
  • The footer credits the website builder instead of carrying your brand.
02What you get

Four pages, fresh film, one simple system

The website

The Overlooks, told whole

A cinematic front door plus a full page for each venue: Walker's, Dulany's, and finally Serenity. Your logo, your reviews, your transparent pricing, and a Check Your Date flow that emails you instantly and sends the couple a warm reply the moment they ask. You have already seen the direction; the concept is a working preview of exactly this.

Shot on your farms

Golden-hour film and photography

I photograph and film weddings for a living. Both Maryland properties get a dedicated shoot: cinematic hero film, fresh galleries, the drone view down the tree-lined drive. Everything on the site belongs to your farms and no one else's.

Optional

Matterport 3D tours

Couples walk the farmhouse, the barn, and the suites from their couch, the same technology I deliver for commercial clients. Especially powerful for out-of-state couples comparing venues from afar.

Yours to run

A simple admin page

Change pricing, swap photos, update dates yourself, no webmaster required. The same setup my other clients use, including America's oldest ultramarathon.
