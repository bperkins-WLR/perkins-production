The barn market

Market days in the same barn since 1940

The barn went up in 1940, held together with wooden-pin construction, and it has been the heart of the farm ever since. Today it is where the fruit comes in from the rows and goes out in paper bags, alongside sparkling cider, nuts, and jellies.

No warehouse, no middleman. Just a barn that has smelled like ripe peaches every summer for more than eighty years.

Three generations

A century and counting on Pryor Road

1905

The family farm begins on this ground as a dairy operation, the first of three generations to work it.

1940

The barn is raised with wooden-pin construction. The farm market still runs out of it today.

2008

The orchard earns GAP food-safety certification, and has held it ever since.

Today

The dairy cows are long gone, the fruit trees took their place, and the third generation keeps the rows picked.

Now imagine it filmed

A 120-year-old orchard is the easiest film we will ever shoot

Every panel on this page is a stand-in, and that is the point. The finished site opens with photography and film made at the orchard by Perkins Production: the barn doors swinging open at eight, baskets on the scale, the rows going gold at the end of the day.

An orchard against a mountain does not need a slogan. It needs a camera pointed at it, once, properly.

The fruit sells itself in person. The finished site lets it do that online.
